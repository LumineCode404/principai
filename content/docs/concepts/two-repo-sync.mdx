The library exists in English, Indonesian, and Chinese. This page records how they stay in sync — the decision, the alternatives considered, the propagation mechanism, and the costs. It is a decision record: written to be checked against reality, not admired.

## Chosen model

- **The EN repo is the single source of truth.** The website and the library live in one repository; `content/docs` is the English canon. Every meaning-bearing change starts there.
- **The ID and ZH trees are derived.** `translations/id` and `translations/zh` mirror the EN principle tree with `source_hash` drift detection. The ZH archive is complete (all principles translated, each page carrying a voice summary); the ID archive is partial and publishes only fully translated files — coverage is stated on its index pages, never implied.
- **One direction only.** Meaning flows from EN outward. There is no two-way merge — see Conflicts and single-repo edits for the one narrow exception, which is not about meaning.
- **The repo itself is English-only.** README, AGENTS, contribution rules, CLI output, and the registry are English; the translated trees are web content derived from the canon.

## Why EN-first

Alternatives, compared honestly:

- **EN-first (chosen).** The source material — papers, postmortems, standards — is English-primary; translating a canon from a translation compounds errors. Agents are most accurate in English, and this library is written for agents. One-way propagation eliminates two-way merge conflicts entirely.
- **Any-language-first.** Rejected: every English citation would become a translation of a translation, and the flow inverts against the language the sources speak.
- **Neutral structure plus translations.** A language-neutral outline with EN, ID, and ZH all derived from it sounds fair. In practice it multiplies the translation work, has no natural reader, and leaves several derived sets drifting with no arbiter.
- **Peer language folders.** Rejected as a structure: peer folders invite peer edits, and two-way merge conflicts return. The chosen model keeps translated trees only as clearly derived output — marked by `source_hash` and `translation_status`, never a peer.

Costs we accept openly:

- **Translation lag.** The derived trees lag EN between syncs. That is normal and made visible by staleness marking, not hidden.
- **Reviewer bottleneck.** One human reviewer grants `reviewed` status. The pipeline queues behind that person; we do not pretend otherwise.
- **Glossary discipline.** Untranslated terms must stay consistent across every file. The [Glossary](/docs/glossary) is mandatory reading for translator and checker alike.
- **Uneven coverage.** ZH is complete, ID is partial, and the website says exactly that on each language's index page. Honesty over symmetry.

## Propagation

1. A change is written in EN first — new principle, edited meaning, corrected citation.
2. The derived counterpart is created from the EN source: frontmatter mirrored, `source_hash` recording the SHA-256 of the EN file at translation time, `translation_status: draft`.
3. A draft may be machine- or agent-translated. That is explicitly allowed — drafts are honest about being drafts.
4. It becomes `reviewed` only after human approval. No exception.
5. Meaning-changing edits go through EN, then re-sync. A translated file never changes meaning on its own authority.

## Drift detection

Two kinds, checked separately by `principai check --against`:

- **Structural parity** — hard requirements, 1:1 with the source: the file list, the ids, the frontmatter keys, the section order, profile membership. A file in EN with no counterpart — or the reverse — is drift. A renamed section is drift.
- **Content staleness** — soft and automatic: when the EN source changes, its `source_hash` no longer matches the hash recorded in the derived file, and the file is marked `translation_status: stale` until re-synced and re-approved.

Structural drift is a broken pipeline. Staleness is expected lag made visible.

## Glossary

Technical terms that stay untranslated — principle, severity, dry-run, blast radius, and the rest — live in the [Glossary](/docs/glossary). The translator consumes it to translate consistently; `principai check` consumes it to reject files that translated what should not be translated.

## Voice summaries (ZH only)

Chinese pages carry a spoken summary — a short audio rendering of the page's own summary text, generated once and shipped as a static asset under `/voice/zh/`. It exists for one language only, deliberately: the language a reviewer can *hear* is the language they can check with their ears as well as their eyes. English and Indonesian pages have no voice; if that ever changes, this page changes in the same commit.

## Enforcement in CI

`principai check` runs in pre-commit and CI:

- **Structural drift is a hard fail.** The pipeline does not pass with a broken structure.
- **Stale translations raise a hard warning or fail**, configurable per repo. Lag is allowed; invisible lag is not.

## Conflicts and single-repo edits

Translation-only edits are allowed for quality — phrasing, register, naturalness. Never for structure (sections, frontmatter shape) and never for meaning. If the meaning is wrong in a derived file, either the canon is wrong or the translation is: fix EN if the canon is wrong, then re-sync and retranslate. Never patch meaning locally.

## Keeping the cost down

What is automated: hash tracking, structural parity checks, staleness marking, voice generation (one script, `bun scripts/gen-voice-zh.mjs`). What stays manual: the actual translation quality and the review approval. Everything mechanical is mechanical; the only irreplaceable human work is judgment.
