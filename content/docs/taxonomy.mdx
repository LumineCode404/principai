This is the living map of the archive's categories. It serves three readers at once: humans browsing the library, agents deciding what applies_to territory they are in, and profiles referencing categories when they select principles. Three rules govern it, and they are non-negotiable:

<Callout type="warn" title="Taxonomy rules">
  No completeness claims. No target numbers. Only the three status words — sudah digali, belum digali, tidak yakin. A category is dug when it is researched with sources and written; dug is not a finish line.
</Callout>

The rules exist because category maps rot in one specific way: they start promising coverage. The moment a taxonomy implies "everything is here," it stops being a map and becomes marketing. This one stays a map.

Some individual principles already touch these areas from inside dug categories — observability-first lives in reliability. A topic appearing above means the category has not been dug as a category; it does not mean the archive is silent on the subject.

The [registry](/registry) complements this map: it is the researched catalog of principles, laws, and heuristics across eighteen domains — each entry with a definition and a source, statuses honest, nothing promised. A registry entry is not a page; a page is earned by digging.

## Tidak yakin

- **Where Law, Principle, and Heuristic blur.** Goodhart's Law is called a law but behaves like an empirical observation; Chesterton's Fence is called a heuristic but is treated as wisdom. The `type` field records our current call, not a settled truth — the boundaries between the three are themselves tidak yakin.
- **Whether practice deserves its own category.** `type: practice` currently lives inside the existing categories. Whether practices — checklists like restore drills and review gates — form a category of their own, or fit better as a type within categories, is undecided. We do not pretend the current shape is final.

## How a category gets dug

Digging is a process with an end that is never an end:

1. Research the field from primary sources — papers, books, postmortems — not from listicles.
2. Write principles one per file, following the [principle format](/docs/concepts/principle-format), each starting `status: draft`.
3. Verify incidents and citations; mark verification honestly — ok or perlu.
4. Human review moves files to reviewed. The category is marked sudah digali when its core is researched and written; digging continues afterwards without changing the word.

The order matters: research before writing, review before reviewed. A category is never marked dug by the volume of files it contains — only by whether the digging happened.

## Proposing additions

1. Write the principle into the relevant existing category — or argue for a new category inside the proposal itself, marked tidak yakin rather than assumed.
2. Mark the file `status: draft` and `verification: perlu`. Every new file starts honest.
3. Follow the [principle format](/docs/concepts/principle-format).
4. Submit for review. `reviewed` is granted only by human approval, and the tables above are updated in the same change.

What is never done: deleting a category because it feels embarrassing to leave it belum digali. The map stays as honest as the archive.
